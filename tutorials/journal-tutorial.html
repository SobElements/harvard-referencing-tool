Journal Referencing Tutorial

Learn how to properly cite and reference journal articles in Harvard style

In-text Citations (Direct Quotes)

▼

When quoting from a journal article, include author, year, and page number:

Format: (Author, Year, p. page number)

Example: "Climate change poses significant risks" (Johnson, 2021, p. 234).

In-text Citations (Paraphrasing)

▼

When paraphrasing journal articles:

Format: (Author, Year)

Example: Recent research highlights climate risks (Johnson, 2021).

Reference List Entry

▼

Journal articles should follow this format:

Format: Author, A.A. (Year) 'Title of article', Journal Name, Volume(Issue), pp.pages.

Example: Johnson, M. (2021) 'Climate change and society', Environmental Studies, 45(3), pp.230-250.

With DOI: Add DOI at the end: doi:10.xxxx/xxxx

Note: The journal name should be in italics (not the title of the article itself)

Online Articles and DOIs

▼

What is a DOI?

DOI stands for Digital Object Identifier.

Permanent: Unlike regular web URLs that can break or change, DOIs are designed to be permanent identifiers which always lead back to the document it links to.

Unique: Each DOI is completely unique to one specific document

Clickable: DOIs function as direct links to the document

DOIs typically look like this: 10.1016/j.edurev.2021.100394

They always start with "10." followed by numbers and letters

With DOI: Johnson, M. (2021) 'Title', Journal, 45(3), pp.230-250. doi:10.xxxx/xxxx

Without DOI but with URL: Include the URL of the journal homepage.

Note: Do not include access dates for journal articles unless specifically required.
