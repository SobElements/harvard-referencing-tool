AI Sources Tutorial

Learn how to properly cite and reference AI tools in Harvard style

⚠️ IMPORTANT - Check Your Institution's AI Policy

Before using AI tools, you must check your institution's AI policy. Some institutions allow the use of AI and others do not. Some also have their own referencing instructions when it comes to AI. Failure to check your institution's policy could lead to an accusation of plagiarism and disciplinary measures as a result.

When to Cite AI

▼

You should cite AI when:

  • Directly quoting AI responses
  • Using AI-generated ideas or analysis
  • Including AI-created content in your work
  • Using AI for data analysis or interpretation

Consider citing when:

  • Using AI for brainstorming (if required by institution)
  • Using AI to check grammar (if substantial changes made)
  • Using AI as a research starting point

In-text Citations

▼

AI citations are still evolving, but here are current best practices:

Direct Quote: (OpenAI, 2024)

Paraphrasing: (Claude, 2024)

General Reference: (ChatGPT, 2024)

Examples:

  • The AI stated that "machine learning is transforming education" (OpenAI, 2024).
  • AI analysis suggests improved efficiency (Claude, 2024).

Reference List Entries

▼

ChatGPT: OpenAI. (2024). ChatGPT (Version 4) [Large language model]. https://chat.openai.com

Claude: Anthropic. (2024). Claude (Version 3) [Large language model]. https://claude.ai

Note: Include the version number when known and the access date if required by your institution.

Best Practices and Ethics

▼

Transparency: Be clear about how you used AI in your work.

Verification: Always verify AI-generated information with reliable sources.

Original Work: Ensure your work represents your own thinking and analysis.

Institutional Policies: Follow your institution's specific guidelines on AI use and citation.

Academic Integrity: Never present AI-generated work as your own original thinking.
