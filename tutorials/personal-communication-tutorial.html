Personal Communications Tutorial

Learn how to properly cite personal communications in Harvard style

In-text Citations Only

▼

Personal communications are cited in-text only and are NOT included in the reference list:

Format: (A. Person, personal communication, Month Day, Year)

Examples:

  • The research methodology was confirmed (J. Smith, personal communication, March 15, 2021).
  • According to the expert interview (Dr. A. Johnson, personal communication, April 20, 2021).

Types of Personal Communications

▼

Personal Interview: (J. Smith, personal communication, March 15, 2021)

Email: (M. Brown, personal communication, April 10, 2021)

Phone Conversation: (Dr. Wilson, personal communication, May 5, 2021)

Lecture/Presentation: (Prof. Davis, personal communication, February 28, 2021)

When NOT to Use Personal Communication

▼

Do NOT cite as personal communication:

  • Published interviews in newspapers/magazines
  • Recorded lectures available online
  • Webinars with public access
  • Social media posts
  • Public speeches with transcripts

These should be cited as their specific source type instead.

Best Practices

▼

Get Permission: Always ask permission before citing someone's personal communication.

Be Specific: Include the full date and type of communication.

Keep Records: Maintain records of the communication for your own reference.

Consider Alternatives: When possible, try to find published sources that say the same thing.
